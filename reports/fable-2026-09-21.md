### A. Yesterday's finding 2 is open in full, and the run before it reported one third of it landed — MEDIUM (carried over)

`crates/mooloop-session/src/take.rs` is a `finish_all`, a `Drop` or a join
site; the only `remove_file` is the `written == 0` branch (`:388-390`), so a

### B. Arming record dirties the document, against the rule written two lines above the call — LOW/MEDIUM

"Not an edit" is spelled in two places and they disagree. The UI's
`on_record_armed_toggled` (`crates/mooloop-ui/src/lib.rs:9262-9278`) says in
its comment that arming "must not make an untouched document look unsaved --
the same rule the transport commands already follow", and sends the
`SetRecordArmed` that `Session::set_record_armed` returns
(`crates/mooloop-session/src/midi.rs:542-548`) down the ordinary command
path. The rule itself lives in `Session::apply_engine_message`
(`crates/mooloop-session/src/engine.rs:691-694`), where `edits` is
`!matches!(command, Play | Pause | Stop)` -- three variants, and
`SetRecordArmed` is not one of them -- so `edits && self.became_dirty()`
(`:701`) marks the session dirty on every arm and disarm. The title takes
its `*` and quit asks about a document nothing changed. A control surface's
transport arm (`midi.rs:1100`) goes the same way. The list at `engine.rs:693`
is the only copy of the policy the code reads; the comment at `lib.rs:9262`
is a copy nothing reads. The test at `engine.rs:882` covers what an install
discards, not what dirties. Plan B.

### C. A refused one-shot command is reported once per process, then never — LOW

`Session::report_refused_command` (`crates/mooloop-session/src/engine.rs:788-800`)
latches `engine_queue_refused` (`session.rs:192`, set at `:792`, cleared
nowhere: three references in the crate) and returns early after the first
refusal. The message it logs is correct -- a one-shot edit "has diverged from
the visible project" -- and is delivered exactly once in the life of the
process, so a second full ring, on a later song, diverges silently. The
window and session copies are written before the send in every caller
(`lib.rs:9270-9276` is one; the pattern is general) and nothing rolls them
back, which the comment at `engine.rs:695-697` already says. Cheapest
honest fix: reset the latch when the queue drains (`EngineHandle::poll`
reports it) or key it per document in `replace_project`; either is one line
and a test. Folded into Plan B.

### E. ML-P8 carries a delay line the seek hook cannot reach — LOW/MEDIUM, audible

Step 03 gave `AudioNode::on_discontinuity` to delay, modulation, reverb and
plate, and `RenderState::on_discontinuity`
(`crates/mooloop-engine/src/render.rs:4536-4543`) fans it out to every live
strip's source and chain and every bus chain; `ChannelStrip` forwards it to
`source_node_mut()` (`:2580-2583`). The eight sources all take the trait's
default (`crates/mooloop-dsp/src/node.rs:337`; no `fn on_discontinuity` in
any of `sampler.rs`, `drumsynth.rs`, `monosynth.rs`, `polysynth.rs`,
`mlm1.rs`, `mlp8.rs`, `ds01.rs`), which is right for seven of them: their
time-state is voices, and a seek still chokes voices. The eighth, ML-P8,
holds a `Chorus` (`mlp8.rs:1675`) that is "one shared `ModulationEffect`"
(`:921-928`) -- the same type that opted in at
`effects/modulation.rs:298` to empty its line on a seek -- and nothing calls
`self.chorus.effect.on_discontinuity`. A chorused ML-P8 keeps its line
running between notes on purpose (`node.rs:513`,
`a_chorused_mlp8_declines_to_rest_between_notes`), so what was in the line
before a seek or a loop fold rings across it, which is exactly the artifact
step 03 removed for the standalone effect. One three-line override and one
test. Plan C.

Two neighbours checked and left alone. `FilterEffect` (`effects/filter.rs:155-167`)
says of itself that its settling time "runs from a few samples ... to
effectively forever as it approaches self-oscillation", and takes the default
hook, so a self-oscillating filter plays through a seek with continuous
phase; under the modulation rule ("free-running state has to arrive at the
same phase whether or not the transport moved") that is arguably correct,
and it is a question for Adam rather than a defect. The console strip
(`strip.rs`) is not an `AudioNode` and is outside the fan-out, which
`docs/AUDIO_ARCHITECTURE.md:254-255` already says.
