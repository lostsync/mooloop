# Team review, 2026-09-22

This review is organised by ownership rather than by subsystem. Adam's question was: *if mooloop were divided among teams, where would the lines be, and what would each team work on first to take it from a hobby project to polished software?*

The earlier daily reviews (`reports/fable-2026-09-17.md` to `-22.md`) looked inward: realtime safety, duplication between Rust and Slint, per-sample cost. This one looks at what a user meets: losing work, loud surprises, clicks, hung notes, silent failures, half-features. Those reports were read first. Where a finding here carries one of theirs, it says so.

**Method.** The app was divided into ten team areas. For each, one reviewer read the source under a common brief: read-only, no cargo, and every claim cited by `file:line` and marked Confirmed or Plausible. The author of this report then re-read the Critical finding and most of the High ones at their cited lines before keeping them, and re-derived two independently (F4 by simulating the smoother in f32; D2 by grepping the workspace for `fsync`). Where two or three reviewers found the same defect independently, the finding says so. The baseline `cargo test --workspace --exclude mooloop-ui` passed (754 s, cloud container). Nothing in `crates/` was changed.

## 1. The teams

Adam's guesses were a MIDI team, DSP working with a devices team, audio, systems/drivers, automation, and timing & tempo. The ten below follow the seams in the code rather than the feature list. Two of the guesses don't hold up:

- **MIDI is not a team here.** It cuts across three layers:
  - port I/O belongs to Platform;
  - notes recorded into patterns belong to Sequencing;
  - learn and CC mapping belong to Parameters & Control, together with automation and modulation, because all three address the same parameter-identity system — the system where this codebase's worst past bugs happened.

  The Control reviewer independently agreed with the three-way split, with one addition: `RenderState::apply_midi`, which decides whether a message is played or mapped, needs a named owner. Today it has none.
- **Timing & tempo is too small on its own.** It is the transport plus the sequencer, and it belongs with patterns and the playlist.

### 4.2 The test infrastructure the teams need

Every team's test gap has the same shape. Units are tested; seams, failures and transitions aren't. Six pieces of shared infrastructure would each unblock several teams:

1. **A null/offline audio driver.** It lets `AppUi` be built in tests (U6), lets the app open without JACK (P2), and gives CI an engine smoke test (P6).
2. **An engine soak harness.** Every device kind, container, send, modulator, lane, take and install, driven through `process_with_input`, asserting no allocation and finite output (R8).
3. **A "control changes are continuous" family.** The largest sample-to-sample step across a fader move, mute, bypass, steal, preset load or loop fold (M2, E2, I1, S3).
4. **A DSP testkit.** Frequency response, alias level, THD and discontinuity checked against the math, at four sample rates (F10).
5. **Document fixtures.** A maximal-project identity round trip, a fixture corpus of songs from tagged releases, a `cargo-fuzz` target on `load_bundle`, and fault injection on the replace path (D10, E11).
6. **An edit-while-playing invariant.** Random edits during playback; every sequencer NoteOn matched within N blocks (S1).

### 4.3 A note on the audit scripts

`dupe-audit unrecorded-edit` reads **0**, and three reviewers independently found that zero means less than it appears to. The check walks `MainWindow` callbacks and macro wirings, but at least seven kinds of edit are applied from the pump: MIDI recording, controller moves, learn binds, sample loads, and generator, preset and kit loads. It is AGENTS.md's own lesson, *"a check is only as honest as its list of places to look"*, arriving at the check that lesson was written about. Extending it to the pump's `mark_dirty`/`dirty = true` sites is S, and it should be done before item 4 is declared finished.

## 5. Per-team detail

Each section gives the charter, a maturity table, the ranked findings with evidence, and the polish gap. Finding ids are the team letter plus a number: R Engine, S Sequencing, M Mixer, I Instruments, E Effects, F DSP Foundations, C Control, D Document, U Interface, P Platform.

### Realtime Engine

**Owns:**
- the realtime callback and its contract: `Executor` (command drain, reclaim ring, backpressure, MIDI decode, load telemetry);
- the `RenderState` block loop as orchestration: discontinuities, idle-skip, channel and track order, takes, preview;
- command application; install and carry (`EngineHandle::install_project`, `carry_plan`, `adopt_performance_state`);
- offline export and capture;
- the `AudioNode` / `EventList` contract.

**Boundary changes the reviewer asked for:**
- Move `core/structure.rs` *out*. It is rack and container document editing, with no realtime content.
- Move the drivers *in*, or give driver lifecycle (panics, server shutdown, sample-rate change, reconnect) an explicit owner. Today nobody owns it.
- Move `session/src/take.rs` in.
- Assign **declicking** of mute, solo and discontinuities to one team. It currently falls between this team and Mixer.

The realtime discipline is well above hobby tier:
- the callback doesn't allocate, and per-thread counting-allocator tests guard that;
- one ordered command stream, with honest backpressure;
- whole states swapped at block boundaries;
- rendering tested to be the same at any block size;
- work time and wake-up gap measured separately.

What's missing is everything to do with **failure**.

### DSP Foundations

**Owns:** the shared primitives in `dsp/src/{filter, biquad, osc, env, lfo, smooth, scale, interpolate, delayline, align, shaper, harmonics, dynamics, analysis}.rs`, the math helpers in `node.rs`, `spikes/time-stretch`, and the composable-units plan.

- boot (`AppUi::new`) and the 8 ms pump;
- document commands, dialogs and error surfacing;
- settings persistence;
- the action registry, shortcuts and gestures;
- theme and appearance;
- pane layout, the status bar and the browser panel;
- the shared controls;
- the UI test harness.

**Boundary change the reviewer asked for:** `lib.rs` is not "the shell". Most of it is the Rust half of every feature team's face: EQ and strip specs, DS-01 values, mixer wiring, sampler and generator wiring. Interface should own the shell modules plus a wiring *framework* (a `Wiring` handle struct and the `wire_*!` macros). Each feature team then owns its `wiring/<feature>.rs` and its view `.slint`. `meter.rs` belongs with Mixer.

The parts that were designed are good. The shared controls agree on drag, fine-drag, double-click reset, wheel, arrow keys and the undo bracket. The action registry is tested against the real markup. Theme derivation is principled and tested. A failed save gets a copyable dialog. What isn't polished is everything *between* the designed parts: guards that a shortcut bypasses, errors that go to a log no desktop user sees, and a constructor that has grown 58% since the plan to split it.

## 7. What this run did not do

- **No cargo command was run by any reviewer.** The one test run was the author's baseline (`cargo test --workspace --exclude mooloop-ui`, green in 754 s). Clippy and the UI tests weren't run. Every cost, level and alias figure is either counted from the code or derived analytically. The reviewers simulated the SVF gain, alias levels, the smoother, and cutoff-vs-rate in Python; the author re-ran the smoother simulation.
- **Nothing was heard.** No finding was checked by ear or on a running instance. "What the user hears" is traced from the code, and several reviewers mark the audible outcome Plausible. The first live check of Tier 1 should be items 7 and 8 on a keyboard.
- **Nothing was run against hardware**: no MIDI controller, no PipeWire restart, no multichannel interface, no macOS device change.
- **No CodeGraph server was available.** The project's configured MCP server refused its connection and no `codegraph_*` tools were loaded, so structural questions were answered by reading.
- **Nothing in `crates/` was changed by the review itself**; the quick wins that followed it are listed in §3. No issues were filed in Linear or GitHub; whether and where this backlog becomes tickets is Adam's decision, alongside the tracker question the documentation pass will raise.
- **Findings from the earlier daily reports** were re-verified only where a reviewer carried them. Three were confirmed **closed** in passing: `fable-2026-09-17` #2 (sampler freeing audio on the callback), `fable-2026-09-21` #4 (ML-P8 chorus and seek) and `fable-2026-09-21` #7 (mixer verbs outside undo).
