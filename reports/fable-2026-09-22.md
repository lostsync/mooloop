## Findings, ranked by impact

### 1. The sequencer has no cursor: every block re-walks every note of the song, and the harness that says the engine is cheap loads one note per channel — HIGH as this grows

`Sequencer::schedule` runs once per span per block (`render.rs:5636`, up to
`MAX_BLOCK_SPANS = 8`), and each run is a walk of the whole store:

- Pattern mode (`sequencer.rs:844-880`): for every active channel, `for note
  in channel.notes().iter()` — every note, filtered only by `start_tick <
  pattern_ticks` — and per note two calls to `schedule_note_edge`
  (`:959-1000`), each doing an `f64` divide, a `ceil`, and a wrap loop, to
  decide whether that edge falls in this block. A block that contains no
  edge at all does the same work as one that contains ten.
- Song mode (`sequencer.rs:888-957`): `for placement in &self.playlist`
  (up to `MAX_PLAYLIST_PLACEMENTS = 512`) × every active channel × every
  note of that pattern, per block, with no test of whether the placement
  covers the block before entering the note loop. The playlist is sorted
  (`sequencer.rs:139,209`) by `PatternPlacement`'s derived `Ord`, which is
  `(pattern, start_tick)` (`playlist.rs:37-42`), so the sort cannot be used
  to prune by time even if someone tried.
- `automation_lane_at` (`sequencer.rs:554-598`) is the same shape for
  lanes: `(0..active_channels).find_map(..)` per destination per block, and
  a full playlist walk in Song mode. `has_automation_at` (`:477-501`) and
  `song_length_ticks` (`:150-162`, another playlist walk, called per block
  in Song mode) exist to hoist one of those walks; the doc comment on
  `has_automation_at` describes the cost it dodges as "descriptor count
  times channel count, squared into the channel count again".

The notes are already sorted by `(start_tick, id)` — `upsert_note` keeps
them so with a binary search (`pattern.rs:145`) — and nothing reads them as
sorted. A `partition_point` on the block's tick window (two ranges when the
pattern wraps, widened by the largest swing offset, `swing_offset_ticks`
`:1020-1027`) turns the on-edge walk into O(log n + k). Note-offs are not
sorted by end tick; a second index per `ChannelPattern`, `(end_tick, index)`,
maintained at the same edit sites, gives them the same shape. The playlist
needs one sort by `start_tick` and one `partition_point` per block.

Why this outranks everything else: it is O(song) per block, at 750 blocks
per second at a 64-frame period, on the one thread that cannot be late, and
every ceiling the product has committed to raises it — `pattern-bank-floor`'s
README already says a longer part means a longer pattern, not a clip. And
the number that currently says "the engine is not a problem" (13-16% of a
quantum on the heaviest document, `pattern-bank-floor/00-status.md`) comes
from `block_cost.rs`, whose projects hold **one note per channel**
(`loaded_project`, `block_cost.rs:48-57`) and whose Song-mode case does not
exist. It measures synthesis. It has never measured scheduling. Plan A.

### 2. Coefficients are recomputed from parameters every sample, in every voice, for values that change once a block — MEDIUM, systemic

The one filter primitive, `Svf::tick` (`crates/mooloop-dsp/src/filter.rs:89-110`),
takes `cutoff_hz` and `sample_rate` and computes `tan()`, a divide, and
three products per call, per stage — and it is called per sample from
fifteen files (86 sites). Around it, each generator derives the cutoff it
passes with more transcendentals per sample per voice:

- sampler (`sampler.rs:1007-1012`): `hz_from_normalized` (`scale.rs:11`,
  a `powf`), a second `powf` for the envelope, then a hand-copied SVF with
  its own `tan` and divide; `apply_drive` (`filter.rs:254-263`) adds two
  `tanh` of which one, the compensation, is a constant of `drive`;
- mono (`monosynth.rs:273-275`), poly (`polysynth.rs:481-483`), ML-M1
  (`mlm1.rs:525`) and ML-P8 (`mlp8.rs:2435`): `hz_from_normalized` per
  sample from a `Smoothed` cutoff (`voice.cutoff.advance()`) that has
  settled to a constant for the whole life of most notes;
- the phaser (`effects/modulation.rs:187-228`): per sample, per stage (up
  to `MAX_PHASER_STAGES = 12`), per channel, two `powf` in `phaser_hz` and
  one `tan` in `allpass_coefficient` — up to 72 transcendental calls per
  sample for one effect instance — where `28.0f32.powf(color)` (`:210`) is
  the same number for all 24 of those calls and for the whole block;
- the compressor's makeup (`effects/dynamics.rs:285`): `db_to_linear` per
  sample of a value automated approximately never;
- the sine oscillator (`osc.rs:221`): `(phase * TAU).sin()` per sample,
  which the module's own header calls naive.

The tree already knows the right shape and uses it in two places: delay
(`effects/delay.rs:149-153,201-202`) and reverb (`effects/reverb.rs:448-461`)
derive the coefficient in `apply_param` and smooth *that*. The rest smooth
the parameter and re-derive the coefficient per sample, which is the
expensive order. The justification written at `filter.rs:20-22,50-51` — an
envelope sweeps cutoff every sample — is true and does not need per-sample
`tan`: the standard answer is to compute the coefficient set at each control
tick (32 frames, the rate the engine already resolves modulation at) and
linearly interpolate `g, a1, a2, a3` across the tick. The SVF is stable
under interpolated coefficients; that is what "topology-preserving" buys.

This is not a crash and not measured as a problem today. It is the reason
the per-voice cost is what it is, and it is where the headroom for a
plugin's worth of DSP per channel will come from. Plan B.

### 3. Automation reaches a device as a stream of step events in the note list, while the engine already has the right representation next to it — MEDIUM (deepens 09-21 finding 5)

A driven parameter is resolved once per control tick and pushed as an
`Event::ParamValue` into the channel's `EventList` (`render.rs:1360-1387`
for effects, `:5917-5936` for sources, `:5967-5990` for routes), by
`push_ordered` (`event.rs:117-131`, an insertion into a 256-slot array,
O(n) per push). The device then splits its block at every event
(`effects/mod.rs:75-91`; the generators have the same loop), so one
automated parameter renders the block in 32-frame pieces, and each piece
re-enters `apply_param`, which for the EQ rebuilds all 19 biquads on both
channels (`effects/eq.rs:79-89`) and for plate and reverb the whole comb
bank (`plate.rs:294-320`, `reverb.rs:427-462`) — every 32 frames.

The capacity is the notes' capacity: `MAX_EVENTS = 256` (`event.rs:70`),
whose comment reasons about a bar of 32nds. At `MAX_BLOCK_SIZE = 8192`
(`bus.rs:15`) one destination emits 256 events and fills the list alone; a
second is silently dropped (`let _ =`, `render.rs:5931,5983`). At 1024
frames, eight destinations do the same. Nothing counts it.

Beside this path the engine keeps two per-tick tables for exactly this
information: `ControlOutputs` (`render.rs:694`, modulator values per tick)
and `StripSegments` (`render.rs:1972-1976`, gain and pan per tick for the
fader). The fader is driven by a table; a filter cutoff is driven by a
sorted list of events that decode into the same table one step at a time.
One representation — a per-destination `[f32; ticks]` curve from a small
pool, handed to the node as a curve — removes the cap, the sort, the
sub-block re-entry and the per-tick `apply_param` cost at once. Events
with sample offsets stay for what they are for (notes, and the plugin
boundary, where CLAP wants them); a curve becomes events only there.
Plan C.

### 4. One window, two device contracts: the generators pay 567 members where the effects pay 32, and every new instrument takes the expensive one — MEDIUM for development cost

`MainWindow` (`crates/mooloop-ui/ui/main.slint:743-7343`) declares 944
callbacks and properties. The eight generator faces (`source-kind == 0..7`,
`:4055-4542`) are bound the flat way — one `in-out property` and one
`callback` on the window per parameter, two-way bound and forwarded by
hand: `filter-cutoff` is declared at `:1818`, its callback at `:1963`, bound
at `:4087`, forwarded at `:4179`, wired in Rust by `wire_unit_param!`
(`lib.rs:11351-11378`) and written back by `refresh_editor`
(`lib.rs:5428`, **252** `window.set_*` calls in one function, 23 callers).
Distinct `root.*` members that block touches: **567**. The fourteen effect
faces (`slot.kind == 0..13`, `:4543-5245`) are rows of one
`for slot in root.effect-slots` over an `EffectSlotRow` struct, and every
one of their parameters leaves through one callback,
`effect-param-changed(index, id, v)` (`:4766-4770`). Distinct members:
**32**. Same window, same author, two contracts, one 18× the other.

The cost is where the product is going. Adding one parameter to an
existing generator is about a dozen sites in six files (face property,
face callback, knob binding, face forwarder, window property, window
callback, instantiation binding, instantiation forwarder, a `wire_*!`
line, a `refresh_editor` line, the core struct field, the DSP read, an
`integrity.rs` clamp). The Rust side has grown 23 `wire_*` macro families
with 205 invocations (`lib.rs:8472-13123`) to keep that bearable. A ninth
generator — the plugin instrument, the layer device's host — starts by
adding its ~140 members to the window, and Slint's generated module (395k
lines, `OPERATIONS.md:135`) grows with every one, because a window
property is code. The effect path already shows the alternative works in
this toolkit: a `SourceRow` struct with the generator's normalized values
and one `source-param-changed(id, v)` callback resolved through the
descriptor table, which is exactly how `effect_slot_row` fills `p0..p17`
by descriptor id (`lib.rs:2418`). Plan E.

Two smaller UI costs of the same kind, recorded here rather than ranked:
selecting a device row calls `sync_effects()` (`lib.rs:10638`), which
rebuilds every slot's row including four per-slot `Vec`s
(`:4325-4343`) and `set_vec`s the model (`:4377`) to flip one `selected`
flag — twenty call sites, most not structural. And the master and input
meters are the only meter writes the pump does not diff
(`lib.rs:15165-15166`, `:15186-15191`): eight `set_*` every 8 ms at
silence, while every other meter face checks `meter_display_changed`
first.

### 5. The pattern axis has no type, and 3,300 lines of `integrity.rs` exist partly to repair what that makes expressible — LOW/MEDIUM, a model decision

`Project` stores patterns as `pattern_lengths: Vec<u16>` (`core/src/project.rs:824`)
and each `ProjectChannel` as `notes: Vec<Vec<NoteEvent>>` and
`automation: Vec<Vec<AutomationLane>>` indexed by pattern (`:535-539`),
with `pattern_meta` a third parallel vector. Nothing types the
relationship; the save-repair pass restores it by hand (`integrity.rs:943-975`,
`:778-789`). The same file's other repairs group by cause the same way:
five `next_*_id` counters stored beside the collections they must stay
ahead of (`:996-1020`); seat-indexed references (`bus` on a channel,
`EffectTarget::Channel(u8)` inside `ParamAddr`) that an edit can repoint
(`:637-657`, `:859-877`, `:1549-1606`); a `kind` tag beside a `source`
enum that already carries it (`:1320-1333`); a container extent stored as
a span over a flat list rather than a tree (`:1358-1380`). Each is a
representation that can hold a state the domain cannot, and a doctor to
put it back.

The pattern axis is the one to name because it is the axis the engine
also pays for: `Sequencer::new` builds `MAX_PATTERNS × MAX_CHANNELS`
`ChannelPattern`s (`sequencer.rs:59-64`, the 1 GiB floor
`CAPACITY_POLICY.md` measures and Adam parked), and finding 1's walks are
walks along it. A `Pattern { length, clips: Vec<Clip> }` with the channel
inside, or a pattern-keyed store, makes the three-vector disagreement
unrepresentable and gives the sequencer a per-pattern index to keep
sorted. It is a document decision before it is an engine one, so no plan
here; `PROJECT_FORMAT.md` is where it would be made.

### 6. Flush-to-zero is x86_64 only — LOW

`enable_flush_to_zero` (`crates/mooloop-engine/src/executor.rs:408-427`)
sets MXCSR FTZ and DAZ under `#[cfg(target_arch = "x86_64")]` and is an
empty function everywhere else. The Core Audio driver is the macOS build,
and a current Mac is aarch64, where the equivalent is bit 24 (FZ) of FPCR
and the function's doc comment — "an order of magnitude longer per
arithmetic op" — silently does not apply. Apple silicon handles subnormals
in hardware at little cost, so this is a gap in the stated contract more
than a measured one; ten lines with `mrs`/`msr fpcr`, in Plan C.

## Architecture

**Three things share one axis.** The sequencer's per-block walk (1), the
document's three parallel vectors (5) and the engine's 1 GiB floor are the
pattern axis expressed as an implicit index three times. Adam's ruling —
patterns stay, no clips — makes the pattern the unit that grows, so the
representation of "what is under the playhead in this pattern" is the one
to get right: a sorted per-pattern store with a cursor serves note-ons,
note-offs and lanes alike, and a pattern-keyed document makes the doctor's
largest category unrepresentable. Do (1) first; it is engine-local and
pays immediately.

**Control signals want a curve, not a list.** The engine resolves
modulation at 32 frames into per-tick tables (`ControlOutputs`,
`StripSegments`) and then re-encodes the result as step events for
devices (3), and the devices smooth the steps and re-derive coefficients
per sample (2). A per-destination tick curve delivered to the node, and a
node that derives coefficients per tick and interpolates them, is one
representation from the modulator to the multiply. It is also the shape a
hosted plugin's parameter queue is built from at the boundary, not before.

**The window is the schema, and it should not be.** 944 members, 567 of
them the generators' (4), each one a line of generated Rust and a line in
`refresh_editor`. The effects show the alternative inside the same file.
Whether the view layer stays Slint or not (`egui-view-layer/` is still
undecided), a generator described by a row struct and a descriptor table
is the part that survives either answer; a generator described by 140
window properties is the part that has to be rewritten under both.

## What this run did not do

- No Cargo command was run; every cost in findings 1-3 is counted from the
  code, and Plans A, B and D each begin with the measurement that would
  have made it a number. `block_cost.rs` has never held a note-dense or
  Song-mode project, so no existing figure contradicts or supports
  finding 1's scaling.
- Finding 2's stability claim (an SVF under interpolated coefficients) is
  standard practice and is not demonstrated here; Plan B's step 2 tests
  it before anything depends on it.
- The FTZ note assumes the macOS target is aarch64; the CI matrix does
  not say which runner architecture `macos-latest` resolves to.
- Nothing about seams, ownership across the callback, undo coverage or
  duplicated constants was re-audited; the five previous reports and
  `scripts/dupe-audit` own those, and this run's brief was to look
  elsewhere.
